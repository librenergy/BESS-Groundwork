## 2026-08-25 (later, energy availability)

* **§2.4 rewritten from the E05 deck, which corrected the draft rather than extending it.** The previous §2.4 was written from the E01 summary table and had energy availability as the weakest of the four ("an EMS estimate carrying SOC error and imbalance"). E05 revises that: energy availability is **measured by a controlled capacity test**, so its accuracy is the *highest* of the four and its update frequency the lowest. Its real blind spot is that capacity tests run in best-case operating conditions, so the result is an upper bound on a bad day.

  **The load-bearing argument is that energy capacity is a slow-moving signal**, changing over months and years rather than intervals, and that every fast energy effect is already captured elsewhere: an offline string by equipment availability (and recovered by rebalancing within minutes anyway), a power derate from SOC imbalance by power availability, a setpoint shortfall from reduced capability by operational availability. Layering an interval-by-interval energy term on top of those **double-counts what they already see**, which is recorded as the most common way an OBE implementation goes wrong. The only dimension nothing else captures is **long-term capacity retention**, the permanent fade no rebalance can fix.

  Added the formula `QA = min(Q_test / Q_guaranteed × 100, 100)%` with both terms defined, and the structural point that **QA enters OBE as a scalar rather than an interval series**, holding its value until a new capacity test is run or the guaranteed value steps down. Noted in §2 alongside the OBE formula so the asymmetry between the three interval series and the one scalar is visible where the combination is defined.

  Added guidance on **reading the test trace rather than its headline number**: the end-of-test discharge taper is **imbalance, not degradation**, and booking it as degradation writes off recoverable capacity; charge energy taken in during the charge taper may not come back out at rated power. Also the point that a re-test pass after a failure is a materially different contractual position from never having failed, so the test history matters as much as the latest value. Settings block §9.1 gained `Q_test`, `Q_guaranteed`, the resulting QA, and the capacity-guarantee step-down schedule with test history.

## 2026-08-25 (later)

* **v0.3: §2 rebuilt on the OBE framework; §3 and §4 re-scoped around it.** Implemented from the maintainer's Module 1 presentation series on BESS availability calculations. This replaces the previous §2, which carried four loosely-defined "equipment measures" inherited from a production generator, with a coherent framework and its arithmetic.

  **OBE, Overall Battery Effectiveness**, is the BESS analogue of manufacturing's OEE. Four availability types, each answering a different question and each blind where the others see: **Equipment** (is it on? full calendar coverage, but on/off only, blind to imbalance, SOC error and derates), **Operational** (is it responding to dispatch? the most accurate of the four since it compares meter to setpoint, but it only exists while dispatched), **Power** (can it deliver now? full coverage, at the cost of trusting an EMS estimate), **Energy** (does it still hold the contracted MWh? weakest data foundation, often only re-based at an annual capacity test). Combined as `OBE = (1/N) Σ min(EA, OA, PA, QA)`: the average of the per-interval **minimum**, because the four are not independent failure modes and the weakest sets the ceiling at every point in time.

  **Each type now carries its computation, not just its definition.** Equipment availability is built bottom-up: `min(contactor, BMS status)` at the string, `min(Σ strings, Σ PCS modules)` at the DC bus because a bus is a series chain, then summed to site with an excused-MW term, with voltage comparisons used as signal quality control rather than as inputs, and a data gap defaulting to zero rather than to last-known. Operational availability gets the shortfall definition (`max(0, |setpoint| − |actual|)`, under-delivery only), the dead-band widened by a ramp rate computed as `min(|Δsetpoint|, |Δactual|)` so the band only opens for ramps the plant genuinely attempted, and the **directional SOC exclusion**, which is the rule most implementations get wrong: a shortfall is excused only when the battery hit a physical limit *in the same direction as the request*, so an empty battery asked to charge or a full one asked to discharge counts fully against availability.

  **Power availability is the section the series exists to fix.** ACP and ADP are kept as two separate numbers from the BMS and PCS function blocks all the way to the final step. The bus roll-up uses `min(String_AXP) × connected strings`, the **weakest** string times the count rather than the sum, because strings on a common DC bus are voltage-locked; summing them is the most common modelling error and it always errs in the owner's disfavour later. The combination is rail-aware: `ADP` at full, `ACP` at empty, `min(ACP, ADP)` everywhere else. Added the derate-cause table (equipment, SOC imbalance, BMS current limiting, PCS temperature, PCS AC voltage, PCS reactive power) and the corner cases to settle in advance (accuracy testing with forced imbalance, idle and standby modes, SOC estimation error at the rails, SOC override for balancing).

  **Capacity normalisation** added as a first-class subsection, since every MW in §2 depends on it: installed vs contracted and the overbuild between them, the fixed-vs-variable contracted-capacity regimes, starting from PCS **apparent-power** rating rather than its active-power label, **directional** losses (losses reduce discharge and help charge, so one loss figure for both directions is wrong in one of them), and converting string energy to power through the sustained C-rate rather than the nominal one.

  **§3 Offtaker reduced to a transcription frame.** The previous draft proposed a model tolling calculation. There is no market standard worth reproducing, so the section is now "based on the executed tolling agreement": a fill-in table for source, level, formula, basis, excused events and who computes it, plus the two questions that decide how much of it the daily report can shadow (does the formula read the outage log or telemetry, and does it measure power only or power and duration). The recurring excused-treatment traps were kept, since those do generalise.

  **§4 Service Provider re-scoped from "here is their definition" to "here is the definition to negotiate in".** The §2.3 power availability calculation is restated as the standard to specify, with the four arguments for it (sees one-sided derates, rail-aware so the usual objection to a plain minimum falls away, rolls up through the real topology, and asks the provider to instrument nothing new), the terms that matter more than the guaranteed percentage (interval and validity rule, directional loss adjustment, capacity basis, owner-side data capture, validation testing), and the standing warning that a provider-computed, provider-archived, untested estimate is not a guarantee. Operational availability remains the dimension usually missing, with the attribution table.

  **Glossary:** OBE, EA, OA, PA, QA, ACP/ADP, and Overbuild added to `definitions.md` §5, with PA flagged ⚠️ for its two competing definitions and SOC amended to warn about basis as well as units.
